Two months ago, I received a mail from the Science Gallery.

“You’ve been accepted for the Quantum Immersive Workshop.”

I genuinely just stared at the screen for a while.
Especially because I had applied literally two minutes before the deadline XD.

I thought it would just be two weeks of learning Quantum Mechanics.

I was completely wrong.

It became two weeks of exploring not just quantum physics, but creativity itself.
How art shapes science.
How imagination precedes discovery.
How every major leap in human understanding first required someone willing to see differently.

For two weeks, I was surrounded by some of the most brilliant people I’ve met — architects, designers, physicists, engineers, artists and thinkers — all approaching reality from completely different directions, yet somehow arriving at the same place.

And the workshop itself felt unreal.

One moment we were discussing quantum reality with Anil Ananthaswamy.
Another moment we were exploring critical artistic practice with Abhishek Hazra, visual language with Roshan Shakeel, cosmology with Spenta Wadia, or watching Rajaram Nityananda casually connect pendulums to Nobel Prize-winning physics.

It never felt like disciplines were separated.

Everything flowed into everything else.

We didn’t just “study” quantum mechanics.

We built spectroscopes by hand.
Played with superconductors and polarizers.
Captured fluorescent, IR and UV photography.
Saw a room-temperature Josephson junction in action.
Visited QPiAI and got to see a 25-qubit quantum computer sitting there like some artifact from the future.

And then came the exhibition phase.

Every team had to create an interactive exhibit inspired by quantum ideas.

The results were beautiful chaos.

Birefringent polarizer cats demonstrating superposition.
Quantum-inspired games.
Installations blending philosophy, physics and visual storytelling into something that honestly felt straight out of a renaissance workshop.

That was the part that stayed with me the most.

Not just the science.
The atmosphere.

Lunch conversations casually jumping between cosmology, philosophy, design and absurd ideas.
People sketching concepts on tissue paper.
Late-night discussions turning into experiments.

I knew I would make friends there.

I just did not expect I’d become close enough with people that, barely days later, we’d be sitting around at 3 AM eating ice cream while debugging code and talking about the universe like we had known each other for years.

That kind of environment changes you.

My biggest takeaway from all this?

The Renaissance never truly died.
We just compartmentalized it.

We separated art from science.
Logic from creativity.
Engineering from philosophy.

And in doing so, we accidentally made learning feel smaller than it really is.

Because the truth is:

The best discoveries happen when disciplines collide.
When curiosity is left unchained.
When you put strange, brilliant people into the same room and simply let them explore.

That is where magic happens.

Huge thanks to @ScienceGallery and @Ajai Naren sir for making this possible and bringing together such an incredible ecosystem of people and ideas.

And thank you to the friends I made there for turning an already unforgettable experience into something genuinely special.
